# Field Notes — What He's Been Saying About Raising

*From his posts on X, December 2025 to November 2026. `principles.md` is how **he** reads a founder;
`raise-playbook.md` is how the **market** reads a raise. This file is the running commentary between the two:
how to tell the story, how to pick investors, which numbers not to juice, and what the seed-to-A squeeze
means for your round. Every point links to the post it came from, so a founder can read it in his own words.*

*These are posts, not essays — short, blunt, sometimes satire. Quote the line, don't stretch it.*

## 1. The story — lead with the end state

- **Ambition is what's being rewarded right now.** His standing fix for a pitch that isn't landing: "what do
  you believe to be true if everything you laid out works? think about the second and third order effects as
  a result of you being successful." Then "put 'this' in the pitch in the first slide and walk the investor
  how you get to 'this' by building what you're building in the first place and how you earn the right to
  get there." The founder who tried it, reluctantly: "it worked." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123066196052381864))
- "Most founders fail to grasp the second and third order effects of the current thing they're building. If
  you just think about those and pitch the end state with a clean path to getting there, your story is a lot
  more fundable. And if you can't think about that, then that's a sign in itself!"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123074656701985207))
- **No single pillar is the pitch.** AI / timing / why now, funding, distribution / hype, market, product,
  revenue — "the best founders treat the following as a necessary component of their business but NOT the
  entire business." Explain how you capture *several* of them in a way that's hard to copy, especially in a
  crowded category, or the VC can't make the case to their own partners. "Please take those ten extra minutes
  to communicate your long term ambition, because that might be the only small difference between you and
  the next round." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2062033620773306763))
- **Three beliefs up top, then a binary call.** "I tell every founder to have a 3 things you need to believe
  in to invest up top and force the investor to commit in a binary way. Usually saves them a lot of time."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073051190636806155))
- **The what, who and why — not the how.** Asked "what" they're building and "for whom," very few people
  can give a straight answer. "Focus on the what, who and why, and not on the how!!"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123521753381232917))
- **Argue both sides.** VCs sometimes ask questions just to test depth — market, technical, obsession. "The
  best founders (imo) are able to argue both sides of an argument with extreme clarity."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))

## 2. What he's actually looking for

- "Founder market fit is all I care about." As a generalist, he needs "that kind of obsession to wake up,
  help move barriers and go work for the founder." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110666132017295612))
- "It's really hard to fake obsession. I don't look at decks. I take walks. If I love a founder I clear my
  calendar and spend the next 24-48 hours with them as much time as they'll give me. This is also why I do
  seed - Series A and not preseed." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110677980376711250))
- On pedigree screeners: "People that look at pedigree, will mostly look at pedigree. You can't really change
  them." He looks for founder-market fit "and that's it."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123265075838333339))
- **Make them use the product first.** "Still waiting on that one founder who gates a VC chat until they
  have played with the product and come with at least two pieces of feedback." Thirty minutes on a parroted
  deck is worse than a product brainstorm — or just getting to know each other.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073903310982218088))
- From his eleven learnings as a VC: "Conviction is never found in the data room." "Naïvety is a virtue, not
  a weakness." "In early stage, pick two: founder, market, product — then believe that the third catches
  up." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042758851074588945))

## 3. Getting in the room — show your work

- "98/100 cold emails I get are pure garbage. Still a lot of alpha out there for being thoughtful, curious
  and using AI judiciously where needed!" ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2090105846810476644))
- Drop "swap notes," "synergies," "pick your brain," "networking," "grab coffee if you're free." Instead,
  **show your work**: "a thoughtful cold email, a five line pointed DM, or complex as some side projects that
  you have shipped, or a meetup that you are organizing." "Even the busiest people have 15 mins to give you,
  if you give them a reason to!" ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119670529254981756))
- Don't read too much into a GP's public thesis — sometimes "the thesis was written and built by an
  associate (or, worse an intern)." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2064175088824717401))
- How fast should a VC reply? "Depends on stage and day. Early for sure." Growth stage, first pitch on a
  Friday, early in the process: "3 days is normal." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2002118981822656659))
- Who to talk to at a firm, the CRM trap, getting to the GP: see `raise-playbook.md` §1 (from his
  [August tips post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2085382457457828153)).

## 4. Picking investors — you get one company, they get a portfolio

- **Find and arm your champion.** "Every (unconventional) deal that's officially wired dies a 100 times in
  venture.. It usually takes one champion sticking their neck out for the founder." Figure out who that is
  and "arm them with whatever info you can to make your case." Associates and non-voting partners are
  useful here — "and it's a good test of how they'd partner with you."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2092079149028716877))
- **Large funds will back your competitors.** Most large-AUM funds "can't afford to miss a category," so
  "they WILL invest in competitors," and seed portfolio companies end up pivoting into the same space. "That
  is the risk you should be wary of when you're choosing your capital partner."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054753629173940522))
- **Hard questions are a good sign.** If an investor consistently asks things that make you really think
  about your business, "try to get them on the cap table!"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))
- **Hardware needs hardware investors.** "I'd never take an investor who hasn't invested in hardware or
  operated in one before... The wrong priors will destroy your company."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117292029160165889))
- **Posting isn't board work.** "'Writes bangers on X, but terrible board member'.. when I recently asked a
  founder about his board." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2056363681798410592))
- **Contracts and a real lawyer.** You can't ask a fund to show conviction and double down when the chips
  are down, and also ask it to skip contracted pro rata because it's convenient for dilution. "Choose your
  partners AND contracts well... Get a good independent lawyer who will spell this out for you."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2109038632975876395))
- "If it's not wired, it's not done yet." "Slow no's are worse than fast no's. Way worse."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042758851074588945))

## 5. The squeeze — timing, price and staying fundable

- **The seed-to-A squeeze.** VCs are mimetic and flock to the hot categories (in mid-2026: AI infra,
  defense, bio, robotics). A good company outside them — a few million in revenue, manageable burn — can
  still raise, but "even at $3 million in revenue, your post is somewhere between $50 and $100 million."
  Seed-to-A graduation rates were once as high as 33% and are "trending under 20%" for newer cohorts.
  ([Series A squeeze](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2070268106564587840),
  [also on his blog](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze),
  [more tips](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117722299638096124)) In October: "is not going to abate
  anytime soon. Never been easier to dream, never been harder to scale."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2113405034826477910))
- **His advice when the term sheet isn't great:** "Run a quick process with the established parties who are
  already interested. Then take it on clean terms IF it meaningfully accelerates your ambition. It's money in
  the bank, and valuation is just a marker in time." The ability to play offense "matters far, far more than
  whether you got the same terms as someone else." ([essay](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2070268106564587840))
- **Be default fundable.** Optimizing only for valuation forgets the 2021–22 vintage: zombiecorns that
  can't raise "even with recaps and >1x liq prefs," and can't be acquired "because no one wants to buy a
  bloated cap table." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2039143855602499629))
- **Capital is a weapon, not destiny.** "Find your unfair advantages. Build a great product with high
  retention and ideally network effects. Use capital as a weapon to compound and not as destiny. And,
  finally figure out how to be a self sustaining business because this money spigot will dry."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2104566122549063756))
- **Venture is a choice.** "No one is asking you to take venture." If you do, know the real options; if
  not, "don't raise venture or go cockroach mode to ride it."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2108420759274369503))
- **Learn the game before you play it.** "VC has effectively fully become vibes capital." Rounds with
  nothing to show close wild; sure shots struggle. "Make sure you understand what's going on before you dip
  your toes in the capital markets." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2083873335998333227))
- **A rejection is learning, not a verdict** — if you're obsessed and don't care what the world thinks.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054599845214650442))
- **Don't benchmark against headlines.** Big rounds are often heavy with SPVs, tranched, and quoting revenue
  that isn't revenue — "don't believe the headlines anymore."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2102534909076349291))
- Founders taking the highest price on offer isn't a grift — "it's just incentive alignment." The VC has to
  do their own diligence. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119212168088522983))
- Two truths: "if you see them make a lot of noise on X, they ARE definitely fundraising," and "every event
  thrown is a sourcing event." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2075635169575469523)) Three more, on the
  hype: everyone wants a $50M seed, everyone thinks they'll hit $30M ARR next year, and "every hot tranched
  seed round magically ends up at the ~$300 million valuation."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2098078391065018816))

## 6. Tranches and your next hire

- **The tell.** "If someone raises xxM $ at under 10% dilution of the published valuation, you can safely bet
  it was a tranched round." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2067397092981772501))
- **Why he's against them:** "it f*cks the next employee that joins." Their 409A is priced off a valuation
  "that the lead preferred investor didn't pay." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2067399657639285150))
- **The triple whammy** for people joining tranched unicorns: strike price often >2x the lead's preferred
  price; a high 409A that makes early exercise expensive; and packages that look great on paper but are
  inflated. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2056125669466333672)) "The early employee or the one joining
  is even more f*cked than the investor. Investor at least has liq pref."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2038696394324906414))
- **Good candidates will check.** His "banana town" checklist tells them to ask: was there a round before
  the last round, and how much went in at the headline price; exactly how revenue is counted, and margins
  ("if they are shy, or don't elaborate, then RUN"); the full options / 409A / preferred / share-count math;
  and that the pref stack gets paid first — a $1B+ company acquired for less than it raised paid founders
  and employees $0. ([checklist](https://x.com/nikunj/status/1997450541023322130),
  [Liar's Valuation](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/liars-valuation),
  [primer](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2000708700823335051)) Even "GPs at VERY good venture firms are twisting
  numbers. It's FOMO and markups." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2000710346223628347)) A higher entry valuation "is often a bug
  and not a feature" for joiners. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2099198567923765357)) A big valuation lets a company make
  bigger offers, but "think about the equity offers from early Miro employees who thought they'd be a hundred
  millionaire but have nothing." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2098191320208793794))
- The exit math is unforgiving: "if you have 100k of stock at 1B in valuation, you need a 10x without
  dilution to hit 1M. And it's so hard to be a 10B company :)"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2000743083898527954))

## 7. Numbers you shouldn't juice

- **Not all ARR is ARR.** Annualized last-month revenue, non-subscription "ARR" with <20% margins, and
  subscription + usage lumped together are different businesses at different multiples. The questions he
  says to expect: what % is annual vs. monthly and how monthly retains; for AI companies, a revenue split
  with exactly how it's calculated, 12-month cohort retention, churn and why; and margins.
  ([peel the onion](https://x.com/nikunj/status/1999850795152617888))
- **Call it what it is.** Anthropic, one of the fastest-growing companies, "has the humility to call it
  'run-rate revenue' on their official announcement... Please don't make up entirely new definitions just to
  call it 'ARR'." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2024499184087552203)) Annualized, inference-driven,
  low-margin, high-churn revenue isn't comparable to a public company's ARR.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2021074607818801256))
- **Bought reach counts against you.** "Paid partnerships and boosting 'views' on X are now negative signal
  in all VC group chats." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2065889759906644146)) Juicing engagement for a
  launch "is having the absolute inverse effect... You're getting completely skewered privately."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054202845356789806)) And never DM VCs a paid "collab" — "shows really
  poor judgement and word spreads around that you are trying to buy eyeballs to cue a big raise."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2108616889605951834)) Paid partnerships are fine; putting the
  impressions in a pitch "as a proxy for demand" is not. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118726380800381144))
- **Launch last, not first.** Most launch videos are "sanitized slop" — dramatic, expensive, "no customer
  anywhere in it." "A launch is the last step, not the first. Find the customer who won't shut up about you,
  point the camera at them and get out of frame. If you can't find an obsessed customer yet, no refinements
  will help." ([Sanitized Slop](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2074984078580031803)) "You still need retention
  before attention." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2089374392295842086))
- **The anti-playbook (satire).** Two posts he wrote as satire, but "individually each line is true" —
  "every sentence in there individually has personally happened to me." Vague-posted revenue with no y-axis, bought articles, LOIs from "friendly"
  customers, cARR, "already oversubscribed," tranching to the moon, no deck because "we're not raising."
  Read them as a list of what not to do.
  ([pre-seed to tranched seed](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118758688567792008),
  [how to FOMO a Series A](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119162212044742938))
- "Momentum is simply everything" has led to "manipulating data, no diligence, growth at all costs."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2034833966835134612))

## How to use this when reviewing a deck

Run these alongside the question bank and the raise-mechanics read:

1. **Slide one:** is the ambitious end state there — with its second and third order effects — and a clean
   path to earning it? Or does the deck open on the how?
2. **The three beliefs:** could a VC name the three things they'd have to believe to invest? If not, offer
   them.
3. **One-pillar pitch:** is the whole case resting on one of AI / timing, funding, distribution, market,
   product, or revenue? Flag it.
4. **Revenue definition:** is "ARR" really annualized, usage-based, or non-recurring? Is it labeled
   honestly (run-rate is fine), and does the deck show retention and margin, or will he have to ask?
5. **Charts and reach:** any chart without a y-axis, impressions or views offered as demand, paid or boosted
   reach, LOIs or contracted ARR standing in for revenue? Each one costs credibility.
6. **Price and tranches:** does the ask imply under 10% dilution at the stated price, or a tranched
   structure? Name the cost: the next hire's strike price, and the next round's markup bar.
7. **Category heat:** if the company isn't in a hot category, does the plan read as default fundable, with
   capital as a weapon and a path to self-sustaining — not one that only works if the next round is a big
   markup?
8. **Investor fit** (when the founder shares who they're talking to): a champion inside each firm? Funds
   with competing portfolio companies? Hardware investors for hardware?
