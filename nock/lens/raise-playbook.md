# Raising — the Playbook

*How the market reads your raise, from his essays and posts (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026). `principles.md` is how
**he** reads a founder; this is how the round gets read before diligence starts. Sections 1–4 change the
deck. Sections 5–7 change how you run the raise. Links go to the source.*

## 1. The story

- **End state on slide one.** Ambition is what's being rewarded. Ask: if everything works, what's true — the
  second and third order effects? Open with that, then show the clean path from what you're building today
  to earning it. "If you can't think about that, then that's a sign in itself."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123066196052381864), [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123074656701985207))
- **Three beliefs up top.** Name the three things an investor must believe to invest, and force a yes or no.
  Each belief needs its proof somewhere in the deck. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073051190636806155))
- **No one pillar is the pitch.** AI/timing, funding, distribution, market, product, revenue: a deck that
  rests on one of them gives your champion nothing to defend to their partners. Show how you capture
  several in a way that's hard to copy. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2062033620773306763))
- **Obvious beats justified.** "As soon as you're justifying the size of your opportunity, you've probably
  already lost." Obvious = the evidence shows it (customers pulling, a market you know from inside).
  Justified = a TAM walk-down asking to be believed.
  ([Series A squeeze](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze))
- **Two of three unfair advantages** — tech, market, GTM (the bar in `principles.md` §2). Three is a home
  run. A competitor already exists; your insight is why you win anyway. Lead with what, who and why; the
  how is the path, not the opener. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123521753381232917))
- **Great hires are the Mandate of Heaven.** Put them high, and say what they unlock. Few founders do.

## 2. The ask and the price

- **You can't take the number back.** Ask $30M, come back at $20M, and you've told the market you couldn't
  raise it and didn't know why you needed it. Aim a tad lower so you close, ideally with competing term
  sheets. Tie the number to the milestones it buys.
- **A lead wants at least 10%.** His rule of thumb, not a law: an established lead rarely takes under
  10% of the company, because a partner has to show how you return the fund. Exceptions need real leverage. Under 10%
  at a big published price usually reads as tranched. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2067397092981772501))
- **Too cheap begets questions.** VCs don't want a "deal"; a low price makes them ask what's wrong.
- **Never argue from a competitor's round.** You don't know how that deal got done. Keep it as a private
  comp; in the deck it tanks the conversation.

## 3. Numbers and signals

- **Say what your revenue is.** Annualized last month, usage, and low-margin non-subscription revenue are
  different businesses at different multiples. Anthropic calls it "run-rate revenue." So should you. Then
  show what he'll ask for next: annual vs. monthly, 12-month cohort retention, churn and why, margins.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/1999850795152617888), [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2024499184087552203))
- **Impressions are not demand.** Organic attention (stars, a #1 launch) is attention: show what it
  converted to. Bought or boosted views offered as demand are a negative signal in VC group chats. Paid
  partnerships themselves are fine.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2065889759906644146), [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118726380800381144))
- **Launch last.** Find the customer who won't stop talking about you, point the camera at them, get out of
  frame. ([Sanitized Slop](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2074984078580031803))
- **Latest full month on the traction slide.** "June numbers in August smell like stale goods."
- **A y-axis on every chart.** Up-and-to-the-right with no scale is on his list of what not to do, with LOIs
  and contracted ARR passed off as revenue. ([satire](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119162212044742938))

## 4. Worked teardown

*Illustrative edits on a fictional seed deck (AI for freight-broker back offices). Rewrites use only facts
in the deck; [brackets] mark what the founder must supply. Do the same in a real review.*

| Slide as written | What he reads | Rewrite |
|---|---|---|
| 1: "AI agents for freight brokerage back offices." | The how. The ambition ("operating system for freight") is buried on slide 10. | Open on the end state: what's true for brokers when Lattice runs their back office. Then the path: documents today → exceptions → [next step] → the operating system. |
| (none) | He can't name the three beliefs. | "1. Brokers pay for this in a down cycle. 2. The exception layer beats the TMS. 3. This team can sell to brokers." Each points to a slide with its proof. |
| 5: "$1.2M ARR (as of July)," chart with no y-axis. | Usage × 12 called ARR, months stale, no scale. | "$[x] run-rate, [latest full month]." Labeled y-axis, monthly revenue, [cohort retention], [gross margin per document]. |
| 6: "Launch video: 3.1M views." | Impressions offered as demand. | Cut the views. "40 inbound demo requests → [n] pilots → [n] paying." |
| 9: Flexport VP Sales, last line. | The best GTM proof, hidden. | Top of the slide: "Built Flexport's broker sales team from 0 to 40." Name it as the GTM advantage. |
| 11: "Competitor X raised $8M at $80M." | The single best way to tank the deal. | Delete. Show what $6M buys: the milestones a Series A needs to see. |

## 5. Who to pitch

- **He underwrites founder-market fit and obsession, not decks.** "I don't look at decks. I take walks."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110677980376711250))
- **Get to the GP** — the partner with real authority, not just the title — through a warm intro, best from
  a founder they backed. "Schedules vanish."
- **Check an associate's standing first.** Look at what they've backed recently. Once a firm logs you under
  someone in its CRM, you're usually theirs.
- **Find your champion and arm them.** Unconventional deals die a hundred times before they're wired; one
  person steers yours through. Give them the material to argue your case inside.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2092079149028716877))
- **Cold works if it shows work:** a five-line pointed note, a shipped side project. Never "pick your brain."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119670529254981756))
- **Make them use the product first**, and come with two pieces of feedback. It beats thirty minutes on a
  parroted deck. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073903310982218088))

## 6. Who to take

- **Most large funds will back your competitors.** They can't afford to miss a category. You get one
  company; they get a portfolio. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054753629173940522))
- **Take the investor whose questions make you rethink the business.**
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))
- **Hardware needs investors who've done hardware.** "The wrong priors will destroy your company."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117292029160165889))
- **Get an independent lawyer, and honor the pro rata you signed.**
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2109038632975876395))

## 7. Running the process

- **Don't lie.** A claimed term sheet is a text away. All decks leak. Share nothing you don't want shared.
- **Slow no's are worse than fast no's, and it's not done until it's wired.**
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042758851074588945))
- **Know the market you're in.** It's consensus: VCs read signals off you and compare notes, so legibility
  is the game. They flock to hot categories (mid-2026: AI infra, defense, bio, robotics). Outside them, a
  good company with a few million in revenue lands at a $50–100M post; seed-to-A graduation is trending
  under 20%. If you're not hot, take clean terms that let you play offense, run a quick process with the
  parties already interested, and treat valuation as a marker in time.
  ([Series A squeeze](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze))
- **Not hot? Pitch the contrarian bet and show a default-alive path.** Portfolios need balance; a champion
  can turn up outside the top 100. Use capital as a weapon, not destiny. Max-price rounds made today's
  zombiecorns. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2104566122549063756),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2039143855602499629))

## Deck checklist

1. **Slide one:** the end state and its second and third order effects, or the how?
2. **Three beliefs:** could an investor name them, and find each one's proof?
3. **Pillars:** does the case rest on one of AI/timing, funding, distribution, market, product, revenue?
4. **Obvious:** shown by evidence, or justified with TAM math?
5. **Ask:** a number tied to milestones, sized to close? Under 10% for a lead, or a "great deal," invites
   questions. No competitor comps.
6. **Revenue:** labeled for what it is (run-rate is fine)? Retention and margins shown?
7. **Signals:** impressions as demand, LOIs as revenue, or a chart without a y-axis?
8. **Traction:** latest full month?
9. **Team:** great recent hires showcased, with what they unlock?
10. **Not hot:** contrarian-bet framing and a default-alive path?
