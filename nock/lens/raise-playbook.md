# Raising — the Playbook

*How the market reads your raise, from his essays and posts (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026). `principles.md` is how
**he** reads a founder; this is how the round gets read before diligence starts. Links go to the source.*

## 1. The market you're raising in

- **It's a consensus market.** "If you're hot, you're really hot. If you're not, you're not." Most VCs read
  signals off you and compare notes. So the game is **legibility**.
- **Never easier to raise a seed, never harder to raise an A.** Seed-to-A graduation was once as high as 33%;
  newer cohorts trend under 20%. No revenue number gets you an A anymore. The bar is whether the
  opportunity is *obvious*: "as soon as you're justifying the size of your opportunity, you've probably
  already lost." ([Series A squeeze](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze))
- **VCs flock.** Outside the hot categories, a good company with a few million in revenue still raises, but
  "your post is somewhere between $50 and $100 million." It's not a slight on your business.
- **Headlines lie.** Big rounds are padded with SPVs and tranches. Don't benchmark against them.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2102534909076349291))

## 2. The story

- **End state on slide one.** Ambition is what's being rewarded. Ask: if everything works, what's true —
  the second and third order effects? Put that first, then show the clean path to earning it. "If you can't
  think about that, then that's a sign in itself." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123066196052381864),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123074656701985207))
- **Three beliefs up top.** Name the three things an investor must believe to invest, and force a yes or no.
  It saves everyone time. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073051190636806155))
- **No one pillar is the pitch.** AI/timing, funding, distribution, market, product, revenue are each
  necessary, none sufficient. Show how you capture several in a way that's hard to copy — that's what lets
  your champion win their partners. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2062033620773306763))
- **Unfair advantages in product, tech, and GTM.** Two of three is the bar; three is a home run. A
  competitor already exists — your insight is why you win anyway.
- **The what, who and why. Not the how.** ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123521753381232917))
- **Argue both sides.** Some questions only test depth. The best founders argue either side with clarity.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))
- **Great hires are the Mandate of Heaven.** Put them in the pitch. Few founders do.

## 3. Who you pitch

- **He underwrites founder-market fit and obsession, not decks.** "I don't look at decks. I take walks."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110677980376711250))
- **Get to the GP** — the partner with real authority, not just the title — through a warm intro, best from
  a founder they backed. "Schedules vanish."
- **Check an associate's standing first.** Look at what they've backed recently. Once you're in a firm's CRM
  under someone, you're stuck with them.
- **Find your champion and arm them.** Unconventional deals die a hundred times before they're wired; one
  person steers yours through. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2092079149028716877))
- **Cold works if it shows work.** 98 of 100 cold emails are garbage, so a pointed, thoughtful one stands out.
  Never "pick your brain." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119670529254981756))
- **A public thesis may not be the GP's.** Sometimes an associate wrote it.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2064175088824717401))

## 4. Who you take

- **Most large funds will back your competitors.** They can't afford to miss a category. You get one company;
  they get a portfolio. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054753629173940522))
- **Take the investor whose questions make you rethink the business.**
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))
- **Hardware needs investors who've done hardware.** "The wrong priors will destroy your company."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117292029160165889))
- **Posting isn't board work.** "Writes bangers on X, but terrible board member."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2056363681798410592))
- **Contracts are trust.** Honor pro rata you signed. Get an independent lawyer.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2109038632975876395))

## 5. The ask and the price

- **You can't take the number back.** Ask $30M, come back at $20M, and you've signed that you couldn't raise
  and didn't know why you needed it. Aim a tad lower, so you close — ideally with competing term sheets —
  and bring a plan for why that amount.
- **VCs don't want a deal.** Too cheap begets "why isn't it higher?"
- **No established lead goes under 10% dilution.** Someone inside is showing how you return the fund.
  Under 10% at a published price usually means a tranched round, and everyone knows it.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2067397092981772501))
- **Never argue from a competitor's round.** "The single best way to tank the deal." Keep it as a private
  comp.
- **Valuation is a marker in time.** If the term sheet is clean and the money lets you play offense, take
  it. Run a quick process with the parties already interested.
  ([Series A squeeze](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze))
- **Be default fundable.** Max-price rounds made the zombiecorns that now can't raise or sell.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2039143855602499629))

## 6. Numbers and signals

- **Not all ARR is ARR.** Annualized last-month revenue, usage, and low-margin non-subscription revenue are
  different businesses. Say what yours is. Anthropic calls it "run-rate revenue." So should you.
  Expect questions on annual vs. monthly, 12-month cohort retention, churn and margins.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/1999850795152617888),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2024499184087552203))
- **Bought reach is a negative signal.** Paid partnerships, boosted views, juiced launch engagement, and
  "collab" DMs to VCs get you skewered in VC group chats. Impressions are not demand.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2065889759906644146),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118726380800381144))
- **Launch last.** Find the customer who won't stop talking about you, point the camera at them, get out of
  frame. No obsessed customer yet? Go back to work. ([Sanitized Slop](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2074984078580031803))
- **Current month on the traction slide.** "June numbers in August smell like stale goods."
- **Charts need a y-axis.** Vague-posted growth is on his list of what not to do.
  ([satire](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119162212044742938))

## 7. Running the process

- **Don't lie.** A claimed term sheet is a text away. All decks leak. Share nothing you don't want shared.
- **Timeline? Two weeks. It's always two weeks.**
- **Loud on X means raising.** Everyone can tell. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2075635169575469523))
- **Be you.** Show what excites you and where you need help. Negging works far less than you think.
- **Make them use the product first** — ideally with two pieces of feedback before the call.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073903310982218088))
- **Not done until it's wired. Slow no's are worse than fast no's.**
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042758851074588945))
- **A rejection is learning, not a verdict.** ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054599845214650442))

## 8. Not in a hot sector

- Pitch **why you're a great contrarian bet.** Portfolios need balance; a champion can turn up outside the
  top 100.
- **Find your default-alive path.** Use capital as a weapon, not destiny; the spigot will dry. If venture
  isn't the fit, go cockroach. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2104566122549063756),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2108420759274369503))
- "All it takes is one yes."

*Unlinked points are from his August 2026 [tips post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2085382457457828153) and
fundraising essay.*

## Deck checklist

1. **Slide one:** the end state and its second and third order effects, or the how?
2. **Three beliefs:** could an investor name them?
3. **Pillars:** does the case rest on one of AI/timing, funding, distribution, market, product, revenue?
4. **Insight:** unfair advantage in two of product, tech, GTM? Presented as obvious, or justified?
5. **Ask:** a number with a plan, sized to close? No sub-10% dilution to a lead, no "great deal," no
   competitor comps.
6. **Revenue:** labeled honestly (run-rate is fine)? Retention and margins shown?
7. **Signals:** any impressions-as-demand, paid reach, LOIs as revenue, or charts without a y-axis?
8. **Traction:** current month?
9. **Team:** great recent hires showcased?
10. **Not hot:** contrarian-bet framing and a default-alive path?
