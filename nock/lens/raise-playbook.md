# Raising: the Playbook

*His fundraising advice in his own words, from his essays and posts (Dec 2025 to Nov 2026). `principles.md`
is how **he** reads a founder. This is how the market reads your raise. Sections 1 to 4 change the deck;
5 to 7 change how you run the raise. Links go to the source.*

## 1. The story

- **End state on slide one.** "Ambition is what's being rewarded right now. What do you believe to be true if
  everything you laid out works? Think about the second and third order effects as a result of you being
  successful." Then: "put 'this' in the pitch in the first slide and walk the investor how you get to 'this'
  by building what you're building in the first place and how you earn the right to get there."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123066196052381864))
- **Work it forward.** What changes for your customer, then for their economics, then for their industry.
  The last answer is the end state. "The OS for X" is a label, not an end state. "Most founders fail to grasp
  the second and third order effects of the current thing they're building. If you just think about those
  and pitch the end state with a clean path to getting there, your story is a lot more fundable. And if you
  can't think about that, then that's a sign in itself!" Stuck? Start with what your best customer could do
  that they can't do today. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123074656701985207))
- **Three beliefs up top.** "I tell every founder to have a 3 things you need to believe in to invest up top
  and force the investor to commit in a binary way. Usually saves them a lot of time." Find them by asking
  what still has to be true once the product works. Each one points to its proof in the deck.
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073051190636806155))
- **No one thing is the pitch.** "The best founders treat the following as a necessary component of their
  business but NOT the entire business: AI / timing / why now, funding, distribution / hype, market,
  product, revenue." Show how you capture several "in a unique way that'll be hard for others to copy...
  Otherwise, it gets very hard for the other side (aka VC) to make a case for their own partners to write
  the check." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2062033620773306763))
- **Obvious, not justified.** There's no revenue number that gets you a Series A anymore. The bar is
  whether what you're building is obvious: "as soon as you're justifying the size of your opportunity,
  you've probably already lost." Obvious means the evidence shows it. A TAM walk-down is justifying.
- **Your unfair advantages.** Share them in tech, market, or GTM, ideally all three (two is the bar in
  `principles.md` §2). There's probably a competitor already doing what you're doing. Your unique insight
  is what tells a VC why you'll win anyway.
- **The what, who and why.** "Focus on the what, who and why, and not on the how!!" The how is the path,
  not the opener. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2123521753381232917))
- **Great hires.** "People severely discount how much hiring great folks gives you the Mandate of Heaven in
  a fundraise... I'm totally shocked how few companies do this." Put them high, and say what's possible
  now that they've joined.
- **Your ambition, in ten minutes.** "Please take those ten extra minutes to communicate your long term
  ambition, because that might be the only small difference between you and the next round."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2062033620773306763))

## 2. The ask and the price

- **You can't take the number back.** "Ask for too much, then they look at you skeptically unless you're
  Elon. Ask for too little and then you're not ambitious enough." Ask for $30M, come back at $20M, and "it
  seeds a doubt that you weren't able to fundraise." Aim a tad lower so you actually close, ideally with
  more than one term sheet, and "be prepared to come up with a resemblance of a plan on why you're raising
  that much." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2085382457457828153))
- **VCs don't want a deal.** "If the price is too good it begets even more questions like why is the price
  not high. It's weird but it's true."
- **Ownership.** "No established good lead VC fund will do under 10% dilution. So don't even ask?
  Obviously there are exceptions and if you have insane leverage go for it. But generally the floor is set
  since someone is showing how this returns the fund." And: "If someone raises xxM $ at under 10% dilution
  of the published valuation, you can safely bet it was a tranched round."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2067397092981772501))
- **Never comp your competitor.** "Never compare on what your competitor got. It's the single best way to
  tank the deal. You have no idea how that deal got done but it's good to have as a comp."

## 3. Numbers and signals

- **Say what your revenue is.** "All 'ARR' is not equal." Annualized last month, usage, and low-margin
  non-subscription revenue are different businesses. "@AnthropicAI, one of the FASTEST growing companies,
  has the humility to call it 'run-rate revenue'... Please don't make up entirely new definitions just to
  call it 'ARR'." Expect the next questions: annual vs. monthly, "retention in 12 month cohorts," churn
  "and why," and margin. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/1999850795152617888),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2024499184087552203))
- **Bought reach.** "Paid partnerships and boosting 'views' on X are now negative signal in all VC group
  chats." "I have no problem with people doing paid partnerships. But when you put it in a pitch and say you
  got these many impressions as a proxy for demand I'm like 💀" Organic attention (stars, a top launch) is
  attention. Show what it turned into. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2065889759906644146),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118726380800381144))
- **Launch last.** "A launch is the last step, not the first. Find the customer who won't shut up about you,
  point the camera at them and get out of frame. If you can't find an obsessed customer yet, no refinements
  will help. Probably just go back to work." ([Sanitized Slop](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2074984078580031803))
- **Fresh numbers.** Your traction slide should show the latest full month. "June numbers in August smell
  like stale goods."
- **A y-axis.** From his satire of the FOMO playbook ("individually each line is true"): "vague post
  revenue. or customers... no y axis, just keep showing numbers and to the right," plus LOIs and cARR from
  "wink friendly wink" customers. Don't. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119162212044742938),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2118758688567792008))

## 5. Who to pitch

- **What he looks for.** "Founder market fit is all I care about." "It's really hard to fake obsession. I
  don't look at decks. I take walks. If I love a founder I clear my calendar and spend the next 24-48 hours
  with them as much time as they'll give me." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110666132017295612),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2110677980376711250))
- **Get to the GP.** "You got to talk to the GP or capital P partners (these are folks who have actual
  authority and not just the title...)... try to get a warm intro. Angels are great for this. Ideally from a
  founder they have already backed. They'll run on a plane to talk to you with that intro. All schedules
  vanish and you don't get passed to an associate (usually)."
- **Check who you're talking to.** "Associates and principals ARE really great... BUT you have to see what
  they have backed recently to understand what kind of leverage they have." And: "once you are assigned to
  someone in the CRM, you're stuck with that person. Wars have been fought on deal attribution (you think
  I'm joking but no)."
- **Find your champion.** "Every (unconventional) deal that's officially wired dies a 100 times in
  venture... It usually takes one champion sticking their neck out for the founder." Arm them "with
  whatever info you can to make your case": your three beliefs, the proof for each, and the hardest partner
  objection with your answer. ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2092079149028716877))
- **Show your work.** "98/100 cold emails I get are pure garbage." "It can be as simple as a thoughtful cold
  email, a five line pointed DM, or complex as some side projects that you have shipped... Even the busiest
  people have 15 mins to give you, if you give them a reason to!"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2090105846810476644), [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2119670529254981756))
- **Make them use it first.** "Still waiting on that one founder who gates a VC chat until they have played
  with the product and come with at least two pieces of feedback."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2073903310982218088))

## 6. Who to take

- **Competitors.** "Most* large AUM fund can't afford to miss a category... This means they WILL invest in
  competitors... they get one company and funds get a portfolio. But that is the risk you should be wary of
  when you're choosing your capital partner." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2054753629173940522))
- **Hard questions.** "There's rarely something VCs ask that should make you *really* think about your
  business. If they do it consistently, try to get them on the cap table!"
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042723383599861817))
- **Hardware.** "I'd never take an investor who hasn't invested in hardware or operated in one before. It's
  just completely different. The wrong priors will destroy your company."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2117292029160165889))
- **Contracts.** "Choose your partners AND contracts well. It's so so so important. Get a good independent
  lawyer who will spell this out for you." ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2109038632975876395))

## 7. Running the process

- **Don't lie.** "If you tell them you have a term sheet from someone, it's literally a text away... this is
  why all decks leak and news spreads like wildfire. And this is why don't share anything you don't want to
  be shared."
- **Fast no's.** "Slow no's are worse than fast no's. Way worse." "If it's not wired, it's not done yet."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2042758851074588945))
- **The market you're in.** "This is the most consensus market I've seen in a while." "VCs are mimetic. We
  follow in flocks into the hot categories. Right now that's AI infra, defense, bio, or robotics" (June
  2026). Outside them, "even at $3 million in revenue, your post is somewhere between $50 and $100
  million." Seed to A graduation rates "were once as high as 33%... for newer cohorts they're trending
  under 20%." ([Series A squeeze](https://writing.nikunjk.com/p/the-series-a-squeeze))
- **If the terms aren't great.** "Run a quick process with the established parties who are already
  interested. Then take it on clean terms IF it meaningfully accelerates your ambition. It's money in the
  bank, and valuation is just a marker in time."
- **Not hot? Strap in.** "Share what makes you a great contrarian bet. Everyone is looking to balance their
  portfolio so you never know where you find champions even if you strike out of the top 100. Goes without
  saying figure out the default alive path." "Use capital as a weapon to compound and not as destiny." And
  "Be default fundable": look at "the sheer number of zombiecorns right now."
  ([post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2085382457457828153), [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2104566122549063756),
  [post](https://x.com/nikunj/status/2039143855602499629))

## Deck checklist

1. **Slide one:** the end state and its second and third order effects, or the how?
2. **Three beliefs:** could an investor name them, and find each one's proof?
3. **One thing:** does the case rest on one of AI or timing, funding, distribution, market, product, revenue?
4. **Obvious:** shown by evidence, or justified with TAM math?
5. **Ask:** a number with a plan behind it, sized to close? Under 10% for a lead, or a "deal," invites
   questions. No competitor comps.
6. **Revenue:** called what it is (run-rate is fine)? Retention and margin shown?
7. **Signals:** impressions as demand, LOIs as revenue, or a chart with no y-axis?
8. **Traction:** latest full month?
9. **Team:** great recent hires up high, with what they make possible?
10. **Not hot:** a contrarian bet and a default-alive path?
